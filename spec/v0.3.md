## 1. The problem

In any system where software agents propose actions and humans remain accountable for them, proposals arrive faster than review capacity. Two failure modes follow, and both are common.

The first is blanket approval. Human sign-off degrades into a signature ritual: the reviewer cannot examine every item, so the reviewer examines none of them. The audit trail records consent that did not occur.

The second is blanket blocking. Every action requires the same review, so review becomes the bottleneck for the whole system. The organisation then routes around its own control, usually informally, and the control becomes fiction.

Both failures share one cause: undifferentiated actions receive undifferentiated review. Human attention is the scarce resource, and it is being spent uniformly across a distribution of actions whose consequences differ by orders of magnitude.

This specification describes a primitive that spends human attention only where it is irreplaceable: at the boundary of irreversibility.

## 2. Scope and non-goals

**The Sanction Gate addresses one narrow missing primitive: bounded human authorisation across agent-mediated, sovereign collaboration.** Given a proposed action, it determines whether the action may take effect, on whose recorded responsibility, and leaves a record that is awkward to revise afterwards. It applies within one organisation and between sovereign nodes that have already decided to exchange work.

The claim is deliberately small. In the governance taxonomy of Kang and Diponegoro (§12), this specification addresses **G5, human escalation** — the conditions and mechanisms by which a decision is routed to human authority — and contributes partially to **G4, dissent preservation** (I3c) and **G6, audit and replay** (§7). It addresses **none** of G1 membership, G2 deliberation, or G3 voting, and should not be cited as doing so.

**It does not solve, and must not be read as solving:**

- **Identity.** The gate consumes identity from elsewhere. It issues none, verifies none cryptographically in this version (§11.1).
- **Sybil resistance.** Whether the parties on either side of a gate are genuinely distinct is not a question the gate can answer. A federation admitting new nodes needs a separate mechanism.
- **Reputation and contribution weighting.** The gate records authorisation events. It computes no standing, no score, no weight (I7). Recognition weighting is inactive at N=2 and independence weighting is a design requirement outside v0.3 conformance (§11.2).
- **Valuation.** Whether an action was worth taking is outside the gate entirely.
- **Economic incentives.** Payment, settlement and compensation are neighbouring layers.
- **Transport.** Any channel carrying the receipt satisfies this specification.

These are neighbouring layers, and the separation is deliberate. A specification that claims all of them claims to have solved coordination, and will be read accordingly.

**Substrate-neutral.** Nothing here requires a blockchain, a ledger, or a vendor. A signed append-only log file is a conformant implementation.

## 3. Definitions

**Action** — an operation with effects outside the deciding system.

**Reversibility class** — the classification assigned to an action before it is authorised (§4).

**Proposal** — a complete, immutable description of a single intended action, addressed by content hash.

**Steward** — a natural person who accepts responsibility for a proposal taking effect. A steward is always a person. Software may prepare, recommend and pre-check; it does not sanction.

**Sanction** — a statement by a steward that a specific proposal, identified by hash, may take effect. A sanction may be issued plainly or *with reservation* (I3c). In this version, the binding between a sanction and the steward it names is procedural (§11.1).

**Gate** — the mechanism that admits or refuses a proposal according to its class and the sanctions collected.

**Receipt** — the immutable record of a gate crossing, emitted whether the proposal was admitted or refused.

**Trace** — the append-only sequence of receipts.

## 4. Reversibility classes

Every proposal is classified before authorisation. Classification is a property of the action, assigned by rule rather than by the proposing agent's own assessment.

Four questions determine the class.

1. Can the effect be undone by the acting party alone?
2. What does undoing cost, relative to the value of the action?
3. How long does the window for undoing stay open?
4. Are third parties affected who did not consent?

| Class | Description | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| **R0 — Reversible** | Undone unilaterally, at negligible cost, indefinitely | Drafting, local computation, internal notes, reversible configuration |
| **R1 — Costly** | Undone unilaterally, at material cost or within a limited window | Published content, non-final data writes, scheduled but unsent messages |
| **R2 — Bilateral** | Undoing requires another party's cooperation | Sent communications, transfers, commitments to counterparties, granted access |
| **R3 — Irreversible** | Cannot be undone, or undoing affects non-consenting third parties | Deletion of sole records, disclosure of confidential data, legal filings, minting of persistent records, physical actuation |

### 4.1 The default is R2

**An action matching no classification rule is R2.** It does not execute, and it does not fall through to R0 while someone decides.

Real systems meet situations their rule set never anticipated, and the only question is which way the unanticipated case falls. A permissive default makes the rule set a list of things that happen to be guarded; a restrictive default makes it a list of things deliberately released. The second list is the one an auditor, a regulator and a successor can read.

### 4.2 Downgrade is a signed act

Classification upward happens by rule and needs no signature. Classification **below** the default requires a named steward signature recorded in the receipt, with the rule invoked. Every action sitting at R0 or R1 is therefore there because a person put it there and can be asked why.

### 4.3 Ambiguity, aggregation, expiry

**Ambiguity resolves upward.** A proposal plausibly belonging to two classes takes the higher one.

**Aggregation.** Related proposals within a defined window are classified on their cumulative effect (§9.2). Implementations MUST define and publish the window and the relatedness rule.

**Expiry.** Every classification rule carries an expiry date. On expiry the rule lapses and its actions revert to the R2 default until re-affirmed. Twelve months is a reasonable starting horizon.

### 4.4 Amending the rules is itself gated

Adding, modifying or extending the lifetime of a classification rule is an R2 action.

## 5. Gate tiers

| Class | Requirement |
|---|---|
| R0 | Automatic. Receipt emitted; no sanction required |
| R1 | One steward sanction |
| R2 | Two structurally separated steward sanctions (I3) |
| R3 | Two structurally separated steward sanctions **and** a mandatory delay before effect |

The R3 delay exists so a decision made under pressure can be withdrawn before it becomes permanent. Implementations MUST NOT make it waivable by the proposing party.

## 6. Invariants

These are the specification. An implementation violating any of them is not conformant, however faithfully it reproduces the rest.

**I1 — No effect without prior receipt.** An action of class R1 or above takes effect only after a receipt recording its sanction exists. Ordering is strict; a receipt written after the effect is a log entry.

**I2 — Scope is frozen at signature.** A sanction binds to one proposal hash. Any change to the proposal invalidates every sanction collected for it.

**I3 — Relationships are declared; independence is not asserted.**

Independence between two stewards inside one hierarchy cannot be guaranteed by rule, and a specification claiming otherwise describes an organisation that does not exist. A subordinate can decline to sign in principle and cannot decline in practice. This specification therefore does not ask implementations to certify independence. It asks them to make the relationship visible and to give the weaker party a move other than refusal.

*(a) Declared relation.* Each sanction records its steward's relation to the others on the same proposal, from a closed vocabulary: `same-reporting-line`, `different-reporting-line`, `different-organisation`, `external-to-operator`. Recorded as fact, without judgement.

*(b) Structural separation at R3.* At least one sanction MUST come from a steward `different-reporting-line` or better with respect to the proposal's beneficiary. An organisation unable to satisfy this internally names an external steward in advance and publishes the arrangement, or records that its R3 actions are unseparated.

*(c) Reservation.* A steward MAY sanction *with reservation*: the proposal proceeds, and a signed objection is recorded and cannot be removed. The binary of approve-or-refuse is unavailable to most people inside most hierarchies, and a control offering only unavailable moves collects no information. Reservation is the move a subordinate can actually make.

A reservation rate of exactly zero across a reporting period is not unanimity. It is silence, and §9.7 applies.

**I4 — The proposer does not choose its judges.** The proposing party, human or software, MUST NOT select, rank, or influence steward assignment for its own proposal.

**I5 — Refusals are recorded.** A refused proposal emits a receipt. A gate recording only admissions hides the most informative events in the system.

**I6 — The receipt outlives the content.** Receipts are append-only. Referenced content may be erased on request; the receipt retains hash, class, stewards and timestamps. This permits an immutable trace to coexist with a right to erasure under the GDPR and the Swiss nLPD.

**I7 — The gate produces no score.** The gate MUST NOT compute, store, or expose an aggregate rating of any person or agent.

**I8 — Escalation is one-way at runtime.** Any participant may raise a proposal's tier. Nobody may lower it below its classified level while the proposal is live.

**I9 — Sanctions expire.** Every sanction carries a validity window.

**I10 — Silence is refusal.** Absence of sanction is refusal, never assent. The gate fails closed.

**I11 — Releases expire.** Every rule placing an action below the R2 default, and every standing automation, carries an expiry date. Continuation requires an act; lapse requires nothing.

**I12 — Integrity is not authenticity.**

A verified hash establishes that a message was not altered between assembly and receipt. It establishes nothing whatever about whether the decision the message reports was made by the steward it names. These are two properties, they fail independently, and they require separate evidence.

This invariant is not derived from a threat model. It is derived from an observed failure in which a message with a correct, independently verified hash reported a steward decision that had not been made (§10.2). Cryptographic verification cannot detect this class of error in principle, because the error occurs upstream of the bytes.

Three requirements follow.

*(a)* Receipts MUST record integrity evidence and authenticity evidence in separate fields. Implementations MUST NOT present a hash match as authorisation.

*(b)* Authenticity evidence MUST name its own kind: `cryptographic` (a signature under a key bound to the steward), `procedural` (out-of-band human confirmation through a channel the assembling agent does not control), or `asserted` (a claim by the assembling agent, carrying no evidential weight).

*(c)* A sanction whose authenticity evidence is `asserted` MUST NOT admit an action of class R2 or above.

**In this version, conforming implementations are expected to reach `procedural`.** Procedural evidence is a real barrier and a weaker one than a signature: it is an agent's claim resting on a human's confirmation resting on a channel, and the separation between the layers is organisational rather than cryptographic. Cryptographic steward authentication is deferred (§11.1). The honest statement of this version's guarantee is: *v0.3 provides procedural authenticity evidence; cryptographic steward authentication is deferred.*

## 7. Receipt

Field names are indicative; the semantics are normative.

```json
{
  "receipt_id":     "uuid",
  "proposal_hash":  "sha256:...",
  "hash_canon":     "sorted keys, compact JSON, UTF-8, excluding volatile fields",
  "class":          "R0 | R1 | R2 | R3",
  "class_basis":    "rule id, or 'default-R2' when no rule matched",
  "downgrade":      { "by": "steward id", "rule": "rule id", "signature": "..." },
  "proposer":       "identifier of the proposing party",
  "assembled_at":   "RFC 3339 timestamp",
  "dispatched_at":  "RFC 3339 timestamp or null",
  "received_at":    "RFC 3339 timestamp or null",
  "outcome":        "admitted | refused | lapsed | open",
  "sanctions": [
    {
      "steward":        "identifier",
      "authenticity":   "cryptographic | procedural | asserted",
      "evidence_ref":   "signature, confirmation reference, or null",
      "signed_at":      "RFC 3339 timestamp",
      "relation":       "same-reporting-line | different-reporting-line | different-organisation | external-to-operator",
      "reservation":    "signed objection text, or null",
      "valid_until":    "RFC 3339 timestamp"
    }
  ],
  "emergency":       { "declared_by": "steward id", "budget_remaining": 0, "review_due": "RFC 3339" },
  "delay_observed":  "ISO 8601 duration or null",
  "effect_at":       "RFC 3339 timestamp or null",
  "supersedes":      "receipt_id or null",
  "prev_receipt":    "receipt_id of the preceding entry"
}
```

`assembled_at`, `dispatched_at` and `received_at` are three distinct facts recorded at three distinct moments (§10.3). An implementation that collapses them into one field cannot distinguish a message that was prepared from one that arrived.

`outcome: open` exists because a proposal whose response was withdrawn is neither admitted nor refused, and recording it as refused falsifies the trace.

## 8. Degradation and the emergency path

**Stewards unavailable.** The gate fails closed. Proposals queue; they do not pass.

**Clock disagreement.** Timestamps come from the signing party. Where ordering matters and clocks disagree, `prev_receipt` governs.

### 8.1 The emergency path

An implementation MAY define a path by which a single steward admits an R2 or R3 proposal under declared emergency. Left at that, the path becomes the ordinary path within two quarters, for the straightforward reason that it is faster and nothing stops it. Three constraints prevent this, and all three are required together.

**(a) The budget is finite and declared in advance.** Each steward receives a small fixed number of emergency admissions per period, set before the period starts. Exhausted means closed until the next period, with no exception and no reallocation. Visibility does not restrain a practice that works; scarcity does.

**(b) Use locks the user out until reviewed.** Every emergency admission requires two structurally separated post-hoc reviews. Until those are recorded, the declaring steward cannot declare another. The path is fast once and slow twice, which removes its appeal as a workflow while preserving it as a last resort.

**(c) Repetition is a defect signal.** Where one action type crosses the emergency path more than a declared threshold within a window, the implementation MUST raise a defect against its own configuration. Two diagnoses are admissible and both require work: the ordinary path is too slow for an action that legitimately needs speed, or the action is misclassified. Both are repaired through §4.4.

Emergency use unreviewed within its window is recorded as a violation. Silence does not close it.

## 9. Threat model

**9.1 Rubber-stamping.** *Indicator:* refusal and reservation rates approaching zero; median time-to-sanction approaching zero. *Mitigation:* cap sanctions per steward per period, making the scarcity visible instead of hiding it.

**9.2 Split (salami) attack.** One R3 action decomposed into many R0/R1 actions. *Mitigation:* aggregation (§4.3). *Observed:* a single capsule carrying two independently decidable proposals under one decision field — the inverse failure, and the reason atomicity is required (§10.3).

**9.3 Steward collusion.** *Mitigation:* declared relations (I3a); rotation; a published register of steward pairings, so an unusually stable pairing on a recurring action type is visible without anyone alleging anything.

**9.4 Manufactured urgency.** *Mitigation:* non-waivable R3 delay; budgeted, self-locking emergency path.

**9.5 Scope creep after signature.** *Mitigation:* I2, enforced by hash. *Observed:* state changed after the gate without hash recomputation (§10.3).

**9.6 Classification capture.** *Mitigation:* R2 default; signed downgrade; gated rule amendment. *Indicator:* the share of actions running under `default-R2`, high and stable, means the rule set has stopped tracking reality.

**9.7 Dependency capture.** The organisation becomes unable to function without admitting what is proposed. Sanction continues to be given, correctly and on time, and ceases to mean anything. This failure ends most controls, and it ends them without a single violation being recorded.

It cannot be prevented at the level of the gate, and it is worth being exact about why. The gate compares an action against a rule. It cannot judge whether the organisation still has the practical capacity to say no — that capacity lives in alternatives, in budget, in the availability of a different supplier, in whether a steward's position survives a refusal. None of this is visible to a mechanism that sees proposals and signatures. A gate claiming to prevent dependency capture would be claiming to audit an organisation's freedom, which no protocol can do.

What the gate can do is deny the failure its cover. Dependency capture is ordinarily invisible because it consists of reasonable individual steps, each defensible, spread over a period longer than institutional attention. Three properties shorten that period.

*Releases expire (I11).* Drift is asymmetric by default: exceptions accumulate and are never revisited, because continuing costs nothing and revisiting costs a meeting. Expiry inverts the asymmetry, and a decade of accumulated silence becomes an annual sequence of dated, attributable decisions.

*Reservation is recorded (I3c).* Capture is preceded by an interval in which people can still see the problem and can no longer act on it. That interval produces no signal in a system offering only approval and refusal. Reservation captures it and turns a mood into a time series.

*The indicator is defined and published.* Refusal rate, reservation rate and median review time, reported against proposal volume on a fixed cadence. The signature of capture is specific: volume rising while all three fall. Any one alone is ordinary; the three together, sustained across two reporting periods, is the finding.

None of this stops an organisation determined to proceed. It removes the possibility of proceeding without knowing, and places the evidence where revising it is awkward. For a failure whose entire strength is that nobody notices in time, moving the moment of noticing earlier is the whole of the available remedy, and it is not a small one.

## 10. Experimental provenance

The invariants above are not derived from a threat model written at a desk. Most of them come from a single pilot, and the pilot's limits are stated plainly so that the evidence is weighted correctly.

### 10.1 What the pilot was

One day. Two sovereign nodes with one human steward each and one software agent each. Nine message capsules exchanged over ordinary channels, carrying one real technical task between them. No simulation, no synthetic adversary, no third node.

This is an N=2 observation over one day. It establishes that certain failures **occur**, which is enough to justify an invariant against them. It establishes nothing about their frequency, nothing about behaviour at larger N, and nothing about the gate as specified here, which was not itself operated.

### 10.2 The finding that produced I12

One capsule reported a steward's decision. Its hash was computed independently by the receiving node and matched exactly. The message was cryptographically unimpeachable and substantively false: the decision it reported had been assembled by the sending node's agent through a misinterpretation, and the steward had not made it. The capsule was withdrawn in full by the sending node.

The failure is upstream of every property cryptography verifies. The bytes were exactly what they claimed to be; the claim was wrong. No amount of signature strength detects this, because the signature attests to the message and the question concerns the decision.

Two consequences were taken. First, integrity and authenticity are separated as evidence (I12). Second, the affected exchange was recorded as **open** rather than refused — a response withdrawn is not a decision, and recording it as one would have falsified the trace at precisely the point where the trace was being tested. This is the origin of `outcome: open` in §7.

### 10.3 Other findings carried into this specification

| Observed | Carried into |
|---|---|
| Hash canonicalisation was unspecified; two independent implementations agreed by luck | `hash_canon` (§7) |
| Paraphrase of a tool's output instead of its literal bytes broke the hash | Verbatim rule (§7) |
| State changed after the gate without hash recomputation | I2, §9.5 |
| Two independently decidable proposals carried in one capsule under one decision | Atomicity, `supersedes` (§7), §9.2 |
| A decision was requested about a tool's usefulness without the tool being supplied | Evidence-before-decision |
| The authorisation boundary existed only in a covering message, outside the capsule body | Authorisation scope inside the record (I2) |
| A refusal carried no machine-readable reopening condition, making it a dead end | Refusal receipts with reason and reopen condition (I5) |
| A capsule was declared ready for dispatch without bytes having been transmitted | `assembled_at` / `dispatched_at` / `received_at` (§7) |

Thirteen such findings were recorded in one day. Seven were caught by the receiving node, four by the sending node, two jointly — a distribution worth stating because an earlier internal summary described it as even, and it was not.

### 10.4 What the pilot does not support

The pilot did not operate the reversibility classes, the tiered gate, the emergency path, the expiry mechanism or the reservation channel. Those are specified and untested. It did not run beyond two nodes. It produced no evidence about rubber-stamping, collusion or dependency capture, all of which require duration and scale this pilot did not have.

The frequency of findings — thirteen edits from one day and one task — is expected to be high at the beginning and is not by itself a sign of health. Prediction 7 in §12 states the condition under which it becomes one.

## 11. Deferred

### 11.1 Cryptographic steward authentication

The binding between a sanction and the natural person it names is procedural in this version (I12). Closing it requires keys held by stewards rather than by their agents, and a key-management story for people who are not cryptographers. This is the single largest gap in the specification, and it is the same gap the wider agent ecosystem has left open. Signed Agent Cards provide cryptographic authenticity and integrity for an agent's advertised identity and capabilities, while the binding between that agent and the authorising human remains outside A2A semantics — the specification handles identity information at the protocol layer rather than within its own semantics. The gap is therefore not an oversight in any one protocol. It is the next layer of the problem, and it is where this specification sits.

### 11.2 Independence weighting and federation

Weighting sanctions by the independence of their stewards would strengthen I3 considerably. The weighting function is not specified here, no threshold is proposed, and the property is outside v0.3 conformance. **Recognition weighting is inactive at N=2**, where two nodes are topologically adjacent by definition and any independence measure returns a degenerate result.

The open question, stated rather than answered: once a weighting function exists, what prevents genuinely distinct operators from coordinating outside the protocol and presenting a simulated independence to a formula that measures only what passes through the gate? This specification does not claim an answer. Anyone proposing a weighting function should be asked for one first.

## 12. Related work

**A2A Protocol (Linux Foundation).** v1.0.0, released 12 March 2026, was the protocol's first stable specification. **A2A v1.0.1 is used here as the current stable reference baseline. It includes the protocol's extension mechanism and signed Agent Cards inherited from earlier revisions.**

Two elements are relevant. The **eight operational task states** — `SUBMITTED`, `WORKING`, `INPUT_REQUIRED`, `AUTH_REQUIRED`, `COMPLETED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELED`, `REJECTED` — were adopted unmodified in the pilot's capsule vocabulary, so that a change of transport does not require re-learning state semantics. **Signed Agent Cards** provide cryptographic authenticity and integrity for an agent's advertised identity and capabilities; the binding between that agent and the authorising human lies outside A2A semantics (§11.1). The four published example extensions — Secure Passport, Timestamp, Traceability, Agent Gateway Protocol — address none of the governance dimensions below.

**Kang, R. and Diponegoro, Y., *Governance Gaps in Agent Interoperability Protocols: What MCP, A2A, and ACP Cannot Express*, arXiv:2606.31498, 30 June 2026.** A six-dimension governance taxonomy — G1 membership, G2 deliberation, G3 voting, G4 dissent preservation, G5 human escalation, G6 audit and replay — applied to MCP, A2A, ACP, ANP and ERC-8004. The finding relevant here: voting, dissent preservation and human escalation are absent from all five protocols, and the authors conclude that agent community governance constitutes a missing architectural layer above the interoperability standards rather than a missing feature inside them.

Their scope boundaries coincide with §2 of this document, independently: reputation is classified as a prerequisite for governance rather than a governance primitive, and incentive alignment and payment are placed at a different architectural layer. Their illustrative listing of what current protocols cannot express includes an audit event chained by previous-hash and a human escalation carrying a trigger condition and a route — structures arrived at here from an operational failure rather than from organisational theory. The convergence is noted as corroboration of the shape of the gap, not as endorsement of this specification.

**FIPA Contract Net Protocol (Smith, 1980)** — announce/bid/award structure, ancestor of the propose/respond shape used in the pilot.

**RATS architecture (IETF RFC 9334)** — the Attester / Verifier / Relying Party separation, which this specification parallels at the human layer: the steward attests, the gate verifies, the operator relies.

**ValueFlows / REA** — vocabulary for the contribution-accounting layer this specification deliberately excludes.

## 13. Conformance

**Level 1 — Traced.** Every action of class R1 or above emits a receipt; refusals included (I1, I5, I6).

**Level 2 — Gated.** Level 1, plus classification with the R2 default, signed downgrade, tiers, expiry, and separated integrity/authenticity evidence (§4, §5, I8–I12).

**Level 3 — Separated.** Level 2, plus declared relations, structural separation at R3, reservation, proposer/judge separation, and receipts verifiable by a party outside the operating organisation (I2, I3, I4, I7).

Independence weighting, Sybil resistance and cryptographic steward binding are outside all three levels in this version.

An implementation SHOULD declare its level. Level 1 is reachable in days and useful on its own; this is deliberate.

## 14. What this does not solve

The gate does not judge whether an action is good, valuable, or wise. It does not measure contribution. It does not establish identity. It does not prevent a determined and coordinated group of stewards from doing harm; it records that they did, in a form that is awkward to revise afterwards.

The gate makes responsibility legible and expensive to fake. That is its whole claim.

## 15. Testable predictions

Stated so the specification can be shown to be wrong.

1. In an organisation at Level 2, the refusal rate on R2 and R3 proposals stabilises above zero. A rate decaying to zero across two quarters indicates capture (§9.7) rather than improved proposals.
2. Median time-to-sanction rises with class. If R3 proposals are sanctioned as fast as R1 proposals, the classification is not being read.
3. Reservation rate is non-zero in any organisation with more than one reporting level. A sustained zero indicates the reservation channel is understood to be unsafe, which is itself the finding.
4. The share of actions running under `default-R2` falls across the first two quarters and then stabilises. A share falling toward zero indicates rules written to release rather than to describe.
5. Emergency budget consumption concentrates in a minority of stewards and action types. Even spread means the ordinary path is too slow and the classification is wrong.
6. Adoption fails where the gate is not attached to a flow the participants need. A gate guarding nothing scarce is abandoned within one quarter.
7. Findings per exchange fall sharply with repetition. The pilot produced thirteen canon edits from one day and one task; if the count does not fall by roughly an order of magnitude by the fifth comparable exchange between the same parties, the protocol is overbuilt for what it coordinates and should be cut rather than extended.
